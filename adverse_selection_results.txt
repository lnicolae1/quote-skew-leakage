==================================================================================================================================
Adverse selection, measured directly; what does the price do after each MM fill?
==================================================================================================================================
Control arm only, no sniffer. 8 seeds x 604800s (7 days). k=0.17763, C=13.66, gamma=1.3392e-06, toxicity=0.10.

adverse move = -(position sign) * (mid[t-1+H] - mid[t-1]), size-weighted, in $/BTC.
Positive means the price moved against the position the fill created. H=10800s is the
column that matters: the corrected inventory half-life is 1.9-4.1h (7d87911).

  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36     234s   fills   2286   vol   7.24 BTC   adverse@10800s =  +59.240 $/BTC
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82      235s   fills   7604   vol  14.34 BTC   adverse@10800s =  +45.278 $/BTC

1. The adverse move per fill, by horizon; and is it the same at both widths?
==================================================================================================================================
  arm                                       H=60s             H=300s            H=3600s           H=10800s
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36     24.8100+-0.7670    28.6379+-0.9144    56.5071+-2.5351    59.2398+-3.5937  
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82      14.6080+-0.3955    16.7456+-0.4669    38.2780+-2.0243    45.2783+-2.6056  

  Drift control; size-weighted mean raw price change over the same horizons ($/BTC).
  Should be ~0; if not, any buy/sell asymmetry below is partly the value path, not the maker.
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36      0.6852            1.4279            9.3850           34.3540
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82       0.3764            1.0448           11.0475           35.5837

  Base / touch ratio: 1.308 at H=10800s, 1.698 at H=60s.
  These are NOT 1, so a selection effect is real and measurable: a quote sitting ~9x
  outside the touch does catch somewhat more adverse fills. But it is a factor of ~1.3-1.7,
  nowhere near the ~7x shortfall below, and adverse selection of $14.6-45.3/BTC persists at
  the touch where there is no selection to speak of. So the selection effect is real but
  secondary; the dominant fact is that every fill is adverse, at every width tested.

==================================================================================================================================
1b. The bridge to reality; adverse move as a fraction of an unconditional one-sigma move
==================================================================================================================================
  one-sigma over H = sigma * sqrt(H / seconds_per_year) * mid, with sigma = 0.3721 annualised
  (the Phase 3 measurement) and mid = $62000. In a real venue conditional adverse selection is
  a few percent of a one-sigma move. This ratio is the number that says whether this
  simulator's flow is far more informative about the next price move than real flow is.

  arm                             H (s)    1-sigma ($)    adverse ($)      ratio
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36          60          31.81          24.81      78.0%
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36         300          71.13          28.64      40.3%
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36        3600         246.41          56.51      22.9%
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36       10800         426.79          59.24      13.9%
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82           60          31.81          14.61      45.9%
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82          300          71.13          16.75      23.5%
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82         3600         246.41          38.28      15.5%
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82        10800         426.79          45.28      10.6%

  The ratio FALLS with H because the unconditional move grows as sqrt(H) while the adverse
  move barely grows; which is the same fact section 1c measures from the other side.

3. Is it symmetric in q's sign, or is the maker caught long into falls?
==================================================================================================================================
  Split by the side of the fill (which direction it pushed the maker's position):
  arm                          MM bought (now longer)  MM sold (now shorter)      asymmetry
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36       23.9780+-21.3879       92.7716+-26.5337          -68.7935
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82         9.5892+-21.7039       80.9336+-20.1786          -71.3444

  THE SAME split, drift-adjusted. A common drift D over the horizon contributes -D to buy
  fills and +D to sell fills, so it cancels in the overall mean but dominates the split.
  These columns add D back to the buy side and remove it from the sell side; they are the
  ones that answer the symmetry question at the holding horizon.
  arm                            MM bought (adjusted)     MM sold (adjusted)      asymmetry
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36                   58.3320                58.4176        -0.0856
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82                    45.1728                45.3499        -0.1771

  H=60s, where the raw drift is already ~0, as an independent read on the same question:
  arm                                       MM bought                MM sold      asymmetry
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36       24.1101+-0.8933        25.4778+-1.5037            -1.3677
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82        14.2185+-0.7390        14.9924+-0.8723            -0.7739

  Split by the maker's inventory sign at the moment of the fill (NOT drift-adjustable --
  this split mixes both fill sides, so read it with the drift row in section 1 in view):
  arm                              already long (q>0)    already short (q<0)      asymmetry
  BASE  2/k, spread ~$18.36       59.1856+-10.0308       64.7152+-7.0180            -5.5296
  TOUCH  mkt spread ~$1.82        45.5501+-4.9294        46.6347+-5.0986            -1.0846

  Both columns positive and close = symmetric adverse selection: every fill is adverse and
  the maker is picked off in both directions equally. One column much larger = a directional
  mechanism, and the write-up has to say which. These are different findings.
