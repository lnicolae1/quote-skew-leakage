Correction; read before the numbers. Added after zero_abs_timeline.py.

  THE RE_DELETE / NEVER_SEEN split is not a mechanistic distinction.
  It measures elapsed price-grid coverage. ever_seen and last_removed
  accumulate monotonically and are never pruned, so once a price has
  been visited any later zero for it scores RE_DELETE by
  construction. NEVER_SEEN can only come from a price this run has
  not yet reached.

  Evidence: NEVER_SEEN per diff across 07-12, one day at roughly
  constant CREATED/diff; 0.2046 at 00:00 falling to 0.0005 by
  17:00, nearly three orders of magnitude, then bursting back to
  0.4800 and 0.8518 on 07-13 02:00 and 03:00 as the price moved into
  unvisited territory. No market quantity behaves like that; a
  coverage counter filling up does.

  So: section A's split is a property of this WINDOW's length and
  price path, not of the venue; a longer run pushes it toward 100%
  RE_DELETE. Section B's ages are time since that price last saw
  activity, not time since a causally related removal, and they do
  not bear on the mechanism. Section D's repeat counts likewise.

  What survives uncontaminated: the total ZERO_ABS count and rate
  (bucket-independent); SEEN_GONE = 8 of 394,287, an independent
  check that snapshot re-anchors replace the book cleanly; and
  section C's near-touch distances, which hold whatever the bucket.

  Settled elsewhere: zero_abs_timeline.py rules out the collector
  artifact; the three reconnect-adjacent hours on 07-13 sit BELOW
  that day's own average. That eliminates a rival; it does not
  establish the fleeting-order explanation.
****************************************************************************************************
